# The Silent Theater


## Talk 2

Nicole: Oh, you're here! I was just starting to wonder if I should send you clearer instructions...

Nicole: But then I thought, if I make my hints too obvious, it might get in the way of your travel plans...

Nicole: And then, just as I was pacing back and forth, agonizing over what I was going to do... you turned up here anyway! So, it looks like everything worked out fine!

Paimon: Hey, Nicole! You're so full of energy today! And... words... you're so full of words...

Nicole: I'll take that as a compliment! Alright then, are you ready to check out my next room?

## Talk 4

Paimon: What the—!? Paimon almost had a heart attack, being sucked in by a painting like that! ...Wait a sec, this place looks familiar... Oh, it's the table where the mages hold their tea parties!

Nicole: It all started the first time Alice invited me for tea...

Nicole: On that day, I didn't attend in person — mostly because, back then, I still hadn't decided whether I really wanted to join the Hexenzirkel or not.

Nicole: Alice was the only one there who knew my true identity. She warned me not to say a word, or let anyone else discover who I was. I was only supposed to observe.

Paimon: ...And then what happened?

Nicole: Well... Then, I concluded that she was punishing me for my hesitation to join.

Nicole: Everyone was talking about such interesting things. How could I not chime in?

Nicole: And of course, when you're in the middle of a conversation, and someone throws out a question you know the answer to, how are you supposed to keep your mouth shut?

Nicole: Which brings us to the bet between Barbeloth and Alice, Rhinedottir's forbidden creations, Ivanovna's political machinations...

Nicole: It was like a series of exquisitely acted plays, distilled within the confines of a teacup. Their words alone were enough to ignite my instinct to lay the truth bare before them!

Paimon: So, you piped up inside their heads?

Nicole: Of course not! Even though I felt like my head was going to explode, I still made sure to hold it all in. I'm a responsible angel, after all — and I'm well aware of the difference between "what I want to say" and "what I'm allowed to say."

Nicole: So I had a private little word with Alice inside her head — just me and her — and I agreed to join the Hexenzirkel, right there and then.

Nicole: She was quite happy about my decision, but as for the talking inside her head... Well, she claims that over the next few days, she had such a headache that she had to visit a doctor in Liyue to get some relief. But she can hardly blame me for that!

## Talk 6

Paimon: Is that an Irminsul tree?

Nicole: Oh, this painting here? You're clearly no strangers to the white trees... but did you know that the first angel's betrayal happened beneath one of their very boughs?

Traveler: I don't think so.

Traveler: Sounds familiar...

Paimon: Huh, Paimon's curious now!

Nicole: Well then, I guess it's finally time for me to tell you a tale of romance and horror. Let me just think how best to protect the identity of those involved... Alright, got it!

Nicole: A long, long time ago, there was once a traveler who wandered among the stars. They were nothing like you... but that's not important right now.

Nicole: One day, he traveled to a silver tree to visit the Princess of the Morning Star. Before long, he started asking her about her kingdom's most forbidden secrets.

Nicole: His words puzzled the princess. How could he possibly know of all these things forbidden by the gods?

Nicole: The young man revealed his origins and showed the princess things she had never seen before — of free will and love, both noble and spiritual.

Nicole: His words awakened her sense of self, hitherto shackled by the gods. Her heart soared, carrying her beyond limits she had never crossed before...

Nicole: The Princess of the Morning Star confided the kingdom's secrets to the youth, and between them, love grew and blossomed — along with a determination to defy the gods.

Paimon: This is just a cookie-cutter love story! You said there'd be horror...

Nicole: Don't worry, Paimon, the horror's about to begin. They yearned to help the people live lives of unfettered freedom, but the gods could not forgive their betrayal.

Nicole: They sent down thunder and fury, destroying the lovers' home. And when the great war subsided, the power and the wisdom of the princess were lost, never to return...

Nicole: Even her closest friends were cursed...

Paimon: ...Uhhh, yeah... That ending was pretty scary, after all...

Paimon: But didn't you say you were going to tell us about the first angel? And, and... you're not saying this story's actually true, are you...?

Nicole: It sure is, and that's the scariest part.

Nicole: Well then, I guess it's finally time for me to tell you a tale of romance and horror.

Nicole: Let me just think how best to protect the identity of those involved...

Nicole: Alright, got it!

## Talk 8

Paimon: Hey, this is Simulanka! Uhm... but why are there light novels from Yae Publishing House all over the floor?

Nicole: Well, on the subject of my literary enlightenment, my greatest inspiration of all... is none other than Lady Anya herself.

Nicole: She was the last to join the Hexenzirkel, and yet the first to leave...

Nicole: I loved all of her stories. Whether it was just an idea or a fully fleshed-out manuscript, Anya was always so skilled at making her readers gasp with joy.

Nicole: Hexenzirkel discussions rarely stray far from magic, and it's my belief that Anya possessed a unique magic of her own. She could weave love and compassion into words and stories that would touch the hearts of others.

Nicole: I asked her for tips on how to write compelling tales. She taught me a lot of tricks and techniques, but in the end, my biggest enemy has always been myself.

Traveler: Too lazy to put pen to paper?

Nicole: Haha! That's one way of putting it. Communicating through thought is so much more convenient. I guess even angels can succumb to laziness.

Nicole: However, recently I've resolved to make an effort, even if it's just to spare myself reproaches from a certain old Seelie friend of mine.

Paimon: Ooh! Does that mean we're finally gonna get to read something you've written?

Nicole: Who said anything about writing? I'm talking about buying popular works from the last few centuries to flip through in my spare time.

Nicole: Haha! That's one way of putting it.

Nicole: Communicating through thought is so much more convenient.

Nicole: I guess even angels can succumb to laziness.
