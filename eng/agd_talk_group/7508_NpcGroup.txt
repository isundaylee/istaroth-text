# Talk Group: Pisemsky (NpcGroup 7508)

## Talk 0

Pisemsky: Such honest passion... How can it not be called beauty?

## Talk 1

Traveler: What do you mean?
Pisemsky: Oh, I was just reliving last night's performance.
Pisemsky: You see, traditional Snezhnayan opera tends to focus on grand tragedies — filling emotional gaps with overly elaborate lyrics...
Pisemsky: But the performances by Miss Odette and Lady Vodyanitsa discard all that unnecessary excess. Elegant. Truly, what elegance!
Pisemsky: Take Miss Odette's footwork, for example — it is less "dance" than it is "martial form." You follow my meaning, yes?

## Talk 2

Traveler: Goodbye.
Pisemsky: Farewell. I do hope that one day, you too will come to appreciate the discipline and beauty of this art form.

## Talk 3

Traveler: Ah, I get it.
Pisemsky: You have good taste, my friend! Miss Odette's choreography always carries a sense of martial restraint.
Pisemsky: Many performers, when expressing intense emotion, tend to fall into chaotic physical excess... but she is different.
Pisemsky: Some of my fellow critics — though forgive my bluntness, they truly understand nothing — love to compare her with her mother and analyze the similarities between their styles...
Pisemsky: But from a truly professional standpoint, Madame Violetta's style leans toward absolute classical restraint, whereas Miss Odette....
Pisemsky: Ah, never mind. True art should be felt, not explained. What words cannot convey should simply remain in silence!
