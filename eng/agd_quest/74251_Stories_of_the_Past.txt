## Talk 3

(Enjoy teatime)

Neuvillette: Many thanks for the invitation, Miss Guillotin. I presume this pure water was prepared specially for me?
Marionette: Think nothing of it, Monsieur Neuvillette. It's an honor to have the Iudex of Fontaine take time out of his busy schedule to join me for a cup of... water.
Neuvillette: Does the Fatui have some official business that you wish to discuss?
Marionette: Not at all. This is a strictly personal visit. And there's no need to be so formal. I simply wanted to ask you about an old acquaintance.
Neuvillette: An old acquaintance... Ah, you must be talking about Mr. Alain Guillotin? The great Lord-Artificer.
Marionette: Exactly. What kind of person was he? I heard he was your subordinate in his youth.
Neuvillette: Hmm...
Neuvillette: Mr. Guillotin was an earnest and grounded young man. Even after being granted the title of White Cavalier of Protection, he never exhibited one iota of arrogance.
Neuvillette: He spent most of his time studying machines. The world outside his own little bubble was of little interest to him.
Marionette: Huh... So he was like that even when he was younger? I wonder if he was wise beyond his years or simply ignorant of the ways of the world.
Neuvillette: However... He changed dramatically after losing his family and friends.
Marionette: The Tower of Gestalt? And Elynas...
Neuvillette: Indeed. When he and I signed the order to search the tower, he appeared... helpless.
Marionette: It was the estrangement and alienation of the past that led to all that. He must have felt guilty, too.
Neuvillette: Hmm... He became even more taciturn after that, and our interactions grew less frequent.
Neuvillette: At the ceremony where he was given the title of Lord-Artificer, all he said was the following:
Neuvillette: "A quiet, humble life is a surer path to happiness than the blind pursuit of endless perfection."
Marionette: Heh... And that's exactly how he lived in his later years.
Marionette: I guess he wasn't just saying that to the crowd. He was saying it to himself.
Neuvillette: And what do you think about it all, Miss Guillotin?
Marionette: Me? Hmph. I won't pass judgment on his epiphany or his way of life, just as he never told me how I should live my own.
Marionette: But I have no intention of either following his path or of imitating his ideals. I'm my own person.
Marionette: Heh... Thank you. You've helped me understand him better.
Neuvillette: You're very welcome. It's a pleasure to share stories of the past with one of his family.
