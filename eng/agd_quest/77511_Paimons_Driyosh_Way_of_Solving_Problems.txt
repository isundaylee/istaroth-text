Black Screen Text: "How should we quantify mission difficulty and define the standards for commissions adventurers can take?"
"Should the system be strict, or more lenient for flexibility?"
Once a difficulty rating system is introduced, commissions previously all classified as B-rank — such as those nicknamed "The Measure of a Mushroom" and "Emergency" — could differ by as much as 95 points in difficulty.
The downside is that we could prevent 30% to 50% of ordinary adventurers from accessing the latter half of commissions within the same rank, potentially reducing their income, discouraging participation, and harming the Guild's overall morale and potential.
The upside, however, is clear: With concrete numbers in front of them, fewer adventurers would overestimate their abilities and recklessly challenge powerful monsters. Injury rates would drop significantly, and medical costs would also be greatly reduced...
Black Screen Text:

Black Screen Text: Next, a sufficiently large random sample of adventurers would be selected from the three participating branches and divided into six groups with evenly distributed strength and ranks for a pilot program. The existing system and the new mechanics would then be applied to the groups in separate instances. Throughout the trial, the Guild would track metrics such as adventurer efficiency, injury rates, overall resource expenditure, and losses. The two systems will then be evaluated comprehensively to determine which should become our main approach. If necessary, different branches could even adopt different systems.
