# Talk Group: Chasca (NpcGroup 31086)

## Talk 0

Chasca: #We meet again at last, Traveler. How have things been with you lately?

Option 1:

> Traveler: Thanks for asking! How's it been for you in Fontaine?
> Chasca: Honestly? Some good and some bad. Let's get the bad out of the way first...
> Chasca: As I was crossing some open water on my Soulsniper, I drifted a bit too close to the shooting range. The Special Patrol mistook me for an airborne target and let off two volleys in my direction...
> Chasca: Luckily, I was moving fast enough that they couldn't lead the shots properly. Not a single round connected, and Captain Chevreuse arrived in time to call a halt to things.
> Traveler: Talk about rough luck...
> Chasca: Well, only good things followed after that...
> Chasca: I took a few easy turns through the city, taking my time to admire all the exquisite man-made architecture, the elaborate machines and ornaments, and the beautifully intricate clothes.
> Chasca: I'd been a little worried beforehand. The Court of Fontaine is a fortress walled in by stone — I wondered whether the limited sightlines might feel oppressive.
> Chasca: As it turned out, the experience was just the opposite of life in the Flower-Feather Clan. Being surrounded by towering buildings gave me, somehow, a strange and pleasant sense of safety.
> Chasca: Once the wargame's over, I'd like to take a couple more turns through the city.

Option 2:

> Traveler: First, what's it been like working with the Special Patrol?
> Chasca: Well above expectations — and not just by a little bit.
> Chasca: Their discipline is remarkable. Although I can already imagine some of the tribal warriors finding them too rigid, too tied up by rules to fight freely...
> Chasca: ...But after training new recruits whose baseline skills were all over the map, I gradually came to understand.
> Chasca: When individuals don't have overwhelming personal strength, cooperation and discipline are the last guarantee of stable combat power.
> Chasca: So I've had a thought — though it might not be one I can act on—
> Chasca: Bringing Captain Chevreuse back to Natlan with me, and getting her to throw in with the Flower-Feather Clan's warriors.
> Chasca: The Mora question would be tricky, naturally. Fontaine's wages and cost-of-living are both on the higher end... but I'd find a way to put together an offer she'd be happy with — beyond just Mora.
> Traveler: You think the Special Patrol is stronger than the Saurian-riders?
> Chasca: No, this isn't about who's stronger or weaker. The point is to make up for what we lack with what they have.
> Chasca: I'm proud of the Flower-Feather Clan's warriors, and I've never doubted the Saurian-riders' ability to take down any monster.
> Chasca: But it's precisely because I know and trust them that I'm clear on our weak spots. In every battle, we inevitably take a certain amount of casualties.
> Chasca: In the past, we've held together as tribes — each warrior doing their part in battle, our coordination looking decent enough on the surface.
> Chasca: But when it comes to bringing together the processes of "responding to losses," "training efficiently," and "replenishing combat strength" — making warrior teams more resilient...
> Chasca: ...Our friends in Fontaine clearly have a wealth of experience worth studying in depth, so we can grow stronger ourselves.
> Traveler: I think I see what you mean...
> Chasca: Managing teams, designing training programs, giving precise commands... in these areas, Captain Chevreuse must be one of Fontaine's foremost experts, no?
> Chasca: Her professional skills, to a warrior pursuing collective strength, are like... a slab of meat sizzling on the open coals to a starving soul.
> Traveler: She really is essential to Fontaine.
> Traveler: You could absolutely call her one of its key figures.
> Chasca: Mm... a shame, then. Fontaine certainly won't let her go.
> Chasca: Poaching from an allied force isn't exactly something a proper warrior of the tribe should be doing.
> Chasca: Just take it as a not-so-appropriate joke on my part.
