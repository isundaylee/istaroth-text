# Witch's Revelation: Great Youkai's Instruction

You encounter Yae Miko at Barbeloth's fortune-telling booth...


## Talk 1

(Talk to Yae Miko)

Paimon: Oh, look... Miko's over there. Paimon gets the feeling things are about to get stressful...
Paimon: You remember the Mikawa Flower Festival, right? Sure, everything turned out for the best, but it was a real headache to get there...
Traveler: And she made us sign ourselves away.
Paimon: But not this time... Hehe, we're not the ones doing labor, so we definitely won't—
Traveler: Keep your voice down...
Yae Miko: #Well, well... Traveler, Paimon. To think we'd run into each other all the way out here in Fontaine. So, what are you—
Paimon: ...Whoa! M—Miko! Did you hear that just now?
Yae Miko: No, I didn't hear a thing. But judging by your expression, I doubt it was anything... flattering?
Paimon: No, no, it's not like that! We're really happy to see you, Miko. Hehehe... What brings you to Fontaine, anyway?

Option 1:

> Traveler: (Well that was an awkward change of subject...)

Option 2:

> Traveler: (Miko really got us this time...)

Yae Miko: Why else would a poor, overworked Editor-in-Chief like me be here? I'm toiling away on Yae Publishing House business, of course.
Yae Miko: I had just bought a box of new books and was planning to take them back for my editors to study...
Yae Miko: When who should I see but an old friend of mine lingering at this fortune-telling booth for the longest time.
Yae Miko: Back home, she rarely visits the shrine to draw fortune slips, so her sudden interest in fortune-telling was quite the surprise...
Yae Miko: Still, while strolling around the city, I overheard people talking about an unusually accurate fortune-teller out here in the countryside.
Yae Miko: So, I came to get a reading, if only to... satisfy my curiosity.
Paimon: Huh? You're finished already? Why didn't you say so sooner!?
Traveler: What card did you draw?
Yae Miko: My, my, taking such an interest in my future, are we? Hehe, if you want to know, I might be persuaded to let you see. However, you'll have to pay a price.
Yae Miko: Let me think... Ah! How about you sign on with Yae Publishing House? You can compile your adventures into a thrilling novel.
Yae Miko: Following the latest naming trends, we could call it, "I Picked Up Emergency Food at the Start, and Now I'm Invincible." Oh, we're going to sell so many copies!
Traveler: It would need to be a pretty thick book...
Yae Miko: Who cares? As long as it's entertaining, you can make it as long as you want. I once published a light novel called "The Horizon Beyond the Paradox."
Yae Miko: That book was as thick as a dictionary and spanned eight volumes. Then there were three companion guides and six spin-offs — stack them all up and they'd reach the ceiling.
Yae Miko: If you manage to churn out that much, then I, Editor-in-Chief, will edit it all personally. What do you say?

Option 1:

> Traveler: You just want to rush my drafts!

Option 2:

> Traveler: Sorry, but no. I still have adventuring to do...

Yae Miko: Interesting... So, you don't want to see which card I drew, then?
Traveler: I've suddenly lost all interest...
Paimon: Yes! It turns out we're not that—
Yae Miko: Oh, well, here it is.
Yae Miko: According to Lady Barbeloth... it's "reversed Justice."

Option 1:

> Traveler: I didn't see that, I covered my eyes!

Option 2:

> Traveler: I didn't hear that, I covered my ears!

Yae Miko: You don't want to see? Very well... Since you have so little interest in divination, the next time you come for a fortune slip, don't expect me to help you draw.
Yae Miko: Come to think of it, you two have been visiting the shrine quite often lately to hang prayer plaques, haven't you? How about...

Option 1:

> Traveler: That's a great card to draw!

Option 2:

> Traveler: Justice... It's got a nice ring to it!

Paimon: *sigh* Seriously!?
Yae Miko: So it would seem, but alas, the Justice card I drew is reversed... Does that mean I'm some sort of outlaw, Lady Barbeloth?
Barbeloth: The card is named Justice, but it doesn't represent a just or unjust person — at least not in a conventional sense.
Barbeloth: Perhaps it would be better to say... Since it's currently reversed, it represents a "dishonest" person.
Yae Miko: Dishonest? There must be some mistake. I'm always honest when helping these two interpret their fortune slips... Isn't that right?
Paimon: #All Paimon knows is that whenever Miko helps Traveler draw fortune slips, it's always "Great Fortune"...
Yae Miko: #That's because of his good luck. Or at least, his good luck whenever he's by my side.
Barbeloth: Luck... Now there's a fine choice of words. Reversed Justice does indeed symbolize a distorted sort of cause and effect, imbalance, dishonesty... and dissatisfaction.
Barbeloth: As a youkai, you above all should understand. After all, the birth of a youkai is indelibly tied to desire.
Barbeloth: A yearning for something, an uncompleted dream, some unfinished undertaking, the pull of a certain emotion...
Barbeloth: All it takes is the slightest catalyst for these unsatisfied desires to take root and grow, and sprout unexpected offshoots.
Barbeloth: And you, a great youkai of Inazuma, likely have a greater desire than most buried within your heart...
Yae Miko: ...
Barbeloth: But that's the limit of what I can see. After all, you only drew one card. What's more, reversed Justice is known to be good at concealing lies, whether from others or oneself.
Yae Miko: How delightfully mystifying... Sounds just like something a shrine maiden might say when interpreting fortune slips. I certainly wasn't expecting to pick up any new jargon on this trip, but that one is quite good.
Yae Miko: Still, as good as it is, it doesn't really align with our shrine's way of doing things. So, perhaps I'd better not teach it to the shrine maidens after all.
Yae Miko: Anyway, thank you for the divination, Lady Mage. Should you ever have the chance, do pay a visit to the Grand Narukami Shrine and allow me to return the favor. What do you say?
Barbeloth: I'm afraid my hydromancy keeps me far too busy. I simply can't spare the time to dip my toes into other forms of fortune-telling.
Barbeloth: Though your fortune slips sound like something Nicole and Alice would enjoy. I'll be sure to tell them all about you.
Yae Miko: Wonderful. When the time comes, I shall personally preside over the drawing of fortune slips for your friends.
Yae Miko: I assure you, everyone will draw the most auspicious slips.
