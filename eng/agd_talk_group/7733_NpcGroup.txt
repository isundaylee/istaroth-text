## Talk 0

## Talk 1

Traveler: I don't think it's appropriate to sing here...

Mengzhou: Why? Because this is a place for reading? That's exactly why I sing!

Mengzhou: No matter how outlandish the nonsense, as soon as it's inked on paper, people take it as fact. This place is teeming with ridiculous books like that.

Mengzhou: In Lang-Gan, no one would ever write such things. No matter what happens, people simply weave it into a ballad.

Mengzhou: When the poetry collectors sent by our emperor hear these songs, they would transcribe and perform them for the high officials at court.

Mengzhou: The raw emotion held within those songs is far more interesting than these hollow writings.

## Talk 2

Traveler: Goodbye.

Mengzhou: Come to do some reading, have you? Well then, why don't you sit down and listen to a song before you go?

## Talk 3

Traveler: Then why are you here?

Mengzhou: Who knows? Perhaps some arrogant fool thought that preserving "civilization" and "history" was some incredible achievement.

Mengzhou: And so, even I was stuffed in here, filed away as nothing more than a piece of "history" myself.

## Talk 4

Traveler: But written words tend to last a little longer, don't they?

Mengzhou: And what good does that do? Once something is written down, it becomes a dead thing. As time passes, the interpretations get twisted, until all you're left with at last is a handful of ignorant descendants coming to blows over the meaning of a word or two.

Mengzhou: Tsk. Better to just make up stories. At least no one would take them for the truth.
