Jotautas: What the... Trespassers? Here? What's wrong with you people!?

Traveler: This is about more than us versus The Doctor. His actions could have consequences for all of Nod-Krai — and perhaps even your homeland, Snezhnaya.

Jotautas: ...I...

Jotautas: He's... building something — ambitious doesn't even begin to describe it. Feels like this could be the big one. God knows how long he's been planning it...

Jotautas: A while back, he told us he'd be returning to Snezhnaya. We weren't expecting him back so soon... This time, he brought some gemstones with him, or something. Two of them.

Lauma: ...The Moon Marrow spheres. He stole them from their rightful owner, Columbina.

Jotautas: Oh... Lady Damselette? Last I heard, she left the Fatui. Before she went, she told us she'd finished what Her Majesty asked of her, and wished to focus on some personal matters.

Jotautas: I remember thinking that leaving us might not be the best choice. I mean, I always looked up to The Captain the most, and...

Traveler: ...

Jotautas: Back when this all started, the higher-ups dispatched three units to Nod-Krai to execute their plan. They were led by The Doctor, Marionette, and The Damselette respectively.

Jotautas: I was part of the The Damselette's unit to begin with.

Jotautas: She never interacted with us much. In fact, she seemed sorta... detached from everything. Not because she was distracted, it just felt like she was indifferent to the world around her. Like she had no stake in anything that happened.

Jotautas: She always seemed like she was staring off into the distance. Almost as if she didn't see anything worth living for in the present, so she had to look further afield... Ah, that's right — she always used to stare up at the sky.

Jotautas: The Doctor used to stand nearby and watch her. Apparently, they got along well at first. But somewhere along the way, she grew wary of him, and started avoiding him as much as possible.

Jotautas: After she left, everyone in our unit merged with The Doctor's. He's... similarly indifferent to the world — except when it comes to the moons. He seems to have big plans for them.

Jotautas: Right now, obtaining the three moons is his number one priority. He even said... if he can only get his hands on two of them, he'll create a third one himself.

Nefer: How would he create an artificial Moon Marrow? ...Maybe the knowledge he gained from the Hyperborean phantoms has something to do with it?

Flins: Thank you for cooperating. We appreciate the level of detail.

Lauma: I get the sense that you'd serve under Columbina again if you had the chance. Would that be fair?

Jotautas: ...No, she's no longer a harbinger. As a member of the Fatui, my loyalty will always belong to the Tsaritsa.

Jotautas: I'm only telling you all this because I don't completely trust The Doctor. His actions do not align with the goals that first brought us to Nod-Krai.

Traveler: I understand. We'll do everything within our power to stop him.

Traveler: The Knave's unit is based at the Kuuvahki Experimental Design Bureau. Maybe you can seek refuge with them.

## Talk 10

The Doctor: Welcome, one and all. I've been expecting you. I hope my humble research facility is to your liking.

Nefer: Yeah, we really appreciated fighting our way in through a horde of monsters.

Lauma: The gall! You expect us to believe you've just been patiently waiting for us?

The Doctor: Of course I have. Should I be... worried about something? Everything seems to be going quite smoothly so far — and you've shown up right on schedule.

The Doctor: First Sandrone and Arlecchino, now you... Ah, quite a surfeit of guests I've had today.

Nefer: ...

The Doctor: If you were looking for them, I should probably mention that Arlecchino disappeared as soon as she entered the facility.

The Doctor: As for Sandrone, why, she was stood right here only moments ago, demanding answers from me at the top of her voice.

The Doctor: Hardly out of character — she never was one for basic courtesy. But I have to admit, I was a little surprised to see her showing concern for someone besides herself.

The Doctor: Does everyone you associate with undergo such a change in temperament? Speaking as a researcher, I have to say — it is not a change for the better. It threatens one's ability to remain objective.

Traveler: Where is Sandrone?

The Doctor: Well, since you ask...

Marionette: So, you've got your hands on the Moon Marrow, and now you're claiming godhood? Don't you think that's a bit of a stretch, Dottore?

The Doctor: I must protest what you are insinuating with that phrase "claiming godhood."

The Doctor: I am not "claiming" anything — I am stating a fact. You're a researcher. I'm sure you appreciate the distinction between the two.

Marionette: Huh? Wait, you really think you're all that?

Marionette: You'll excuse me if I'm not being a polite little lady for you. We're colleagues — we both know what kind of person you are.

The Doctor: By the same token, I knew you'd respond along those lines.

The Doctor: You and I both understand that facts speak louder than words. To that end, I've had my people send you a small gift. It should go some way towards helping you understand my present position.

Marionette: What gift?

Marionette: ...

Marionette: Where have all your troops gone?

The Doctor: Who knows? Perhaps they are already marching towards a new world, following in the footsteps of an unknown god...

The Doctor: As vulgar and arrogant as you may be, dear Sandrone, I have no intention of taking out my displeasure on your subordinates. They will enjoy equal rights to my own.

Marionette: You sent your people to the Experimental Design Bureau!? That's the Tsaritsa's property!

The Doctor: Yes, and I am very mindful of Her Majesty's instructions. But you know full well that they do not conflict with my present goals.

The Doctor: In which case, all I'm doing is conducting another one of my experiments. Is there a problem with that?

The Doctor: Now then. You have a judgement call to make. Am I simply lying to stall you? Or is it possible that every word I am saying is the truth — in which case, your subordinates really are in danger?

Marionette: So now you've turned on fellow Fatui soldiers? You're completely insane!

The Doctor: I set a new rule for myself: Each person gets one chance. Naturally, that includes you, Sandrone.

The Doctor: If you wish to leave, I won't stop you. We are colleagues, after all.

Marionette: ...Scumbag!

The Doctor: Still, I hadn't counted on Sandrone sparing any thought for the people of this world. Shame... I always found her self-centeredness to be one of her few better qualities.

The Doctor: How can she ever hope to achieve greatness when she's so obsessed with protecting her little design bureau?

Flins: It's more than a little disingenuous to speak of achieving greatness while taking hostages. Or does honor no longer mean anything to you?

The Doctor: Honor?

The Doctor: I have the marrow of two moons in my possession, and will soon complete the construction of a third. I will be the god of a new world... and you speak to me of honor? How charmingly naive.

Lauma: Those are sacred relics! You do not deserve them!

The Doctor: In my view, many in this world do not deserve to be alive — and yet, I mercifully permit them to go on living, do I not?

Traveler: You'll come to regret your actions.

The Doctor: Will I now? Let's see if you can make me.
